# Prompt submitted to Arena (Battle + Code)

Build a polished, fully working, responsive website for Wrenfield Nutrition, a fictional online nutrition coaching practice for U.S. adults who are taking a GLP-1 weight-loss medication (like semaglutide or tirzepatide) or coming off one. Coaching happens by video across the U.S. and works alongside the client's prescriber: the coach never gives medication or dosing advice. Demand is high because about one in nine U.S. adults now takes one of these drugs and many get little nutrition support, but nutrition coaching is crowded with generic macro plans, supplement upsells and big promises. The site must feel fresh, calm, kitchen-real and honest, build trust without hype, and turn visitors into qualified free fit-call requests through a clear funnel. Implement the complete website now in the available environment, not a mockup or plan.

DESIGN DIRECTION
Create a warm, editorial "good kitchen" aesthetic: cream #FBF7F0 background, deep sage #2F4A3A for text and headers, terracotta #C4623F for primary actions, soft wheat #F2E6CF panels, muted olive #7C8B5A accents and a leaf green #3E8E5E for success states. Verify WCAG AA contrast on every actual color pairing. Pair an elegant, characterful serif for headings with a clean, highly readable sans for body (Google Fonts with system fallbacks). Body text at least 17px with 1.6 line height. Use a split hero: headline on the left, a high-quality photo of a balanced, protein-forward plate or a person cooking a simple meal on the right, with a small floating card reading "Eating less? Then every bite has to count." Use a small wren mark, hand-drawn plate-divider lines and leaf sprigs as section accents, not generic gradients, glassmorphism or stock-startup blobs. Use real, relevant images from reliable public image URLs with meaningful alt text, explicit width/height or aspect-ratio, lazy loading below the fold, and a graceful fallback so no broken images appear. Restrained hover and scroll transitions only.

CONTENT AND SECTIONS
1. Slim demo notice bar: "Concept website: fictional business, sample content." Sticky header with a custom wordmark (simple wren mark plus "Wrenfield Nutrition"), nav links: Sound Familiar, Plate Check, How It Works, Plans, Coach, FAQ, and a prominent "Book a free fit call" button. Include a working mobile menu (hamburger, accessible, closes on link click and Escape).
2. Hero headline: "Your appetite changed. Your nutrition plan should too." Supporting copy about one-to-one nutrition coaching that helps people on GLP-1 medications eat enough protein, handle side effects with food choices, protect strength, and build habits that last if they ever stop. Primary CTA "Book a free fit call" opens the funnel; secondary CTA "Take the Plate Check (2 min)" scrolls to the assessment. Three small trust cues without fake credentials: "Works alongside your prescriber", "Clear pricing up front", "No supplements to buy".
3. "Sound familiar?" strip with four tappable cards: "I forget to eat, then feel awful", "Nausea makes most food unappealing", "I'm losing weight but feel weaker", "I'm worried about what happens when I stop". Each opens a short accessible panel with why it often happens, one small thing to try this week (food and habit ideas only), a reminder to talk with their prescriber about symptoms, and a "Work on this with a coach" CTA that opens the funnel with that concern preselected.
4. Interactive "Plate Check" assessment (funnel step 1): 7 single-choice questions shown one at a time with a progress bar, Back button and keyboard support, covering where they are on the medication (just starting / steady / tapering or stopped), meals per day, protein at meals, fluids, fiber and vegetables, strength activity, and how side effects affect eating. It produces a score out of 100 and one of three results: "Build the base", "Fuel the strength", or "Lock in for life". Each result shows a plain explanation, the 3 weakest areas as a mini bar chart, 3 next steps, and a recommended plan, with a "Bring my Plate Check to a free call" CTA that carries the score, result and recommended plan into the booking form. Include retake. State clearly that this is a self-reflection tool, not medical advice or a diagnosis.
5. "What we don't do" honesty panel comparing Wrenfield vs. common red flags (medication or dosing advice, supplement or powder upsells, 800-calorie crash plans, guaranteed pounds-lost promises, before/after photo marketing, pressure to pay on the first call).
6. How it works, 4 steps: Free fit call, Your plate plan, Weekly check-ins, Strong-for-life habits.
7. Plans (3 cards): "Fresh Start" (4 weeks, 3 sessions, sample $290), "Steady Strength" (12 weeks, 8 sessions plus messaging, sample $890, labeled only "Our core program"), and "Taper & Maintain" (8 weeks for people reducing or stopping medication with their prescriber, sample $590). Each card lists who it's for and what's included, and a "See details" button opening an accessible dialog with a session-by-session outline, what you will leave with, what it asks of you, and a CTA that preselects that plan in the booking form. Label all prices as sample. Add a comparison table that turns into stacked cards on mobile. Include a short note that clients can ask about HSA/FSA eligibility with their plan administrator (no promises).
8. "Your kitchen toolkit" deliverables: 4 concrete outputs framed as deliverables, not promises (a personal protein-first plate plan, a "small appetite" meal and snack list, a side-effect-friendly grocery guide, and a 3-day-a-week strength starter routine to discuss with your doctor).
9. Meet the coach: one illustrative fictional coach (clearly fictional name, short story about years helping people cook simple meals and coaching clients through medical weight loss alongside their care team) with a tasteful portrait image, labeled as a sample profile. No fabricated certifications, awards, press logos, statistics, reviews, ratings or testimonials. Instead add an "Our promises to you" panel with 4 concrete commitments (for example, a written plate plan within 48 hours of your first session).
10. Free resource band: "The GLP-1 Protein Plate Guide" email capture (first name, email, a "where are you now" select, consent checkbox) with inline validation and a demo success state.
11. FAQ accordion (8 items): is this medical care, do you tell me how to change my dose (no: talk to your prescriber), I'm not on a GLP-1 yet can I still join, do I have to count calories or macros, what if food makes me nauseous, will I keep the weight off if I stop (honest: no guarantees, we build habits to help), do I need special foods or supplements, refunds and rescheduling.
12. Strong final CTA band ("Eat less. Nourish more.") and footer with nav, plans, session hours labeled as sample, a clear "not medical advice" note, and an accessible privacy-notice dialog. No real phone numbers, emails or addresses: use clearly fake placeholders like (555) 010-0142 and hello@wrenfield.example.

FUNNEL AND UX
Build a multi-step "Book a free fit call" dialog that is the main lead funnel: Step 1 where you are (medication stage: considering / just started / steady dose / tapering or stopped, main concern preselected when opened from a card, how soon you want to start); Step 2 your goals (top goals as checkboxes, eating preferences or restrictions, optional notes on side effects, plan interest preselected from a CTA or Plate Check); Step 3 contact and time (first name, last name, email, optional phone, time zone, preferred day and time window, consent checkbox); Step 4 review summary with Edit links, showing any Plate Check result and selected plan. Show "Step X of 4" correctly on every step, never higher than 4. Next and Submit must be separate buttons so clicking Next on step 3 goes to the review step, never submits. Use persistent labels, autocomplete attributes, required indicators, field-level inline errors, aria-live validation messages, Back/Next buttons and preserved values across steps. Move focus to the first invalid field on error. Final button "Request my call" shows a success state with a summary and next-step timeline plus "Demo only: your request has not been sent." No backend, no data storage, no console logging of form data. Dialogs must have accessible names, a close button, Escape support, focus trapping and focus restoration. Add a sticky mobile bottom bar with "Free call" and "Plate Check" buttons that never covers content. Every button and link must work: no dead links or empty hrefs.

ENGINEERING QUALITY
Semantic landmarks, one h1, logical heading order, skip link, visible focus styles, 44px minimum tap targets, prefers-reduced-motion respected. Mobile-first and verified at 375px, 768px and 1440px with no horizontal overflow. Make sure global element styles never override component colors or make radio buttons and checkboxes stretch to full width. Do not invent statistics, study results, client counts or success rates anywhere. Keep concerns, quiz questions and scoring, plans and FAQs in an easily editable data structure. Optimize images, avoid heavy libraries, include a descriptive page title, meta description, Open Graph tags and favicon. Use the environment's supported stack, ensure it compiles and runs with no console errors, and test the full booking funnel and the Plate Check end-to-end before finishing. Prioritize excellent visual craft, clarity and reliable interactions.
